# One fault, several teams

*People, artificial intelligence and engineering ideas*

> Editorial review edition. The opening story and industrial examples are fictional. Prototype capabilities, plans and production deployments are different stages. Illustrations were created with AI.

## One fault, several teams

Consider a hypothetical industrial example. A worker discovers a pump failure and records it through the established procedure. The shift supervisor needs the immediate situation. The area manager needs responsibility assignments and progress. The chief engineer needs confirmed causes and technical risks. The director needs the consequences, expected timings and decisions requiring management attention.

An assistant could assemble authorised information: the equipment manual, repair history, an approved procedure and parts availability. It could then draft a work request or a concise summary. Each claim should carry its source, currency and a clear status: confirmed, suspected or still being checked.

If a spare part is unavailable, the system may help prepare an enquiry. A qualified specialist must approve any substitute. Emergency actions follow approved procedures. A language model must not invent hazardous instructions or send control commands to equipment.

A useful outcome is a preserved history: who reported the event, who checked the cause, what decision was approved and how the work ended. This is a concept for possible use, not a claim of deployment at any factory.

## From an article to a responsible pilot

An organisation can begin with a limited scope: searching approved documents, drafting maintenance requests and summarising events. Its existing portal retains user management. The assistant receives only the information it needs through authorised interfaces. The public VMESTE website must not gain access to industrial data.

The first pilot can be read-only. Local hosting can support processing without sending documents to a public model, but location alone does not ensure security. Access controls, audit records, backups and checks of what the system reads and stores are still necessary.

Benefits should be assessed against questions chosen beforehand. How long does retrieval take? Is the correct source cited? Can a worker see someone else's restricted documents? Does the model invent the cause of a fault? Can service be restored after a failure? Hardware and user capacity must follow measured workloads rather than a compelling presentation.

After the pilot, specialists decide what to improve and whether to expand. Employees' experience becomes reviewed knowledge, while AI helps retrieve and organise it. Engineering decisions, work authorisations and responsibility remain with people.
