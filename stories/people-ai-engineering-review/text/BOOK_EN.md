# VMESTE. Ideas That Help People

*People, artificial intelligence and engineering ideas*

A continuation of the illustrated series. From an everyday request for help to local AI, engineering models and responsible uses of technology in industry.

> Editorial review edition. The opening story and industrial examples are fictional. Prototype capabilities, plans and production deployments are different stages. Illustrations were created with AI.

## Someone to turn to

That month, everything seemed to go wrong for a young man. His work laptop broke, he had to find another flat, and water appeared beneath the sink just before an important meeting. His phone was full of contacts, but he could not decide whom to ask. He began a message, deleted it, and stared at the screen again.

A neighbour asked what had happened. He wanted to say, “I am sorting it out.” Instead, he wrote, “Honestly, I do not know where to start.” She helped him separate the problems: first the meeting, then photographs of the fault and a clear description of the help he needed. Finding a repairer became easier. He checked the diagnostic fee and specifically asked for his files to be preserved.

A few days later, he knocked on her door: she needed another pair of hands to fit a shelf. Over tea, they discovered that he knew how to lay out documents, and she needed exactly that work. Later, in his new flat, he read someone else's request and replied, “I think I can help.”

This abridged fictional story asks a question for the whole book: how can technology make the first request easier without replacing human involvement with an attractive interface?

## VESTA: helping people explain the task

“Nothing works” can conceal several different needs. Describing symptoms, preserving important files and arranging a time all have to become a clear request. An assistant that asks useful questions and suggests a draft can help with this step.

That is the direction of VESTA. Local development connects request drafting with a reference catalogue. Suggested wording must remain editable. Publishing, choosing a provider and sending a message require a separate user action. A well-written draft does not mean that the job has been done.

A reference listing does not become a registered provider account. The assistant must not invent availability, prices, ratings or travel arrangements. When information is insufficient, it should say so and request clarification. Reference listings retain a warning that participation in VMESTE and availability are unconfirmed.

Quality is assessed through specific situations: was the service identified correctly, was an important restriction preserved, and were excluded records withheld? The value of an answer lies in its accuracy and usefulness, not in how confident it sounds.

## TASHI: a workspace on your own computer

People also need help without opening a website. Instructions, correspondence, drawings and unfinished texts accumulate on a computer. Finding the right document can take longer than writing a new one. A local assistant could provide a convenient entry point to authorised materials.

TASHI is being developed as a separate project. Its areas of work include local AI, voice, documents, files and launching applications. A menu item or experimental module does not establish that every operation works reliably on every computer. Each build needs testing with its actual device and models.

The user chooses accessible folders. Permission to read does not automatically permit overwriting. Deletion, command execution and other consequential actions require separate confirmation. Installed local components may operate offline; downloading models and consulting external sources have their own requirements.

A practical example is finding an instruction manual and preparing questions about it. The answer should identify the document behind each claim. If there is no suitable source, the assistant must not present a guess as something found in a file.

## An engineering idea has to be tested

A diagram can look complete long before a device exists. A model helps investigate an idea: compare operating modes, expose contradictions and develop questions for testing. A coloured temperature indicator does not, by itself, measure anything.

This series includes a local heating simulator. Its values are calculated by software. Such a model can support discussion of zones and responses to changing conditions, but it cannot establish actual building temperatures or energy savings. Those claims require real measurements, suitable instruments and an agreed method.

A circuit board prepared in engineering software must also pass through component checks, manufacturing, testing and documentation. An artistic robot image depicts an idea rather than an assembled machine. That distinction matters to readers and prospective customers alike.

Progress begins with a question that can be checked. Does the software react correctly when a sensor is lost? What does an operator see when something fails? How can the previous version be restored? Answers turn an interesting experiment into a foundation for further engineering work.

## One fault, several teams

Consider a hypothetical industrial example. A worker discovers a pump failure and records it through the established procedure. The shift supervisor needs the immediate situation. The area manager needs responsibility assignments and progress. The chief engineer needs confirmed causes and technical risks. The director needs the consequences, expected timings and decisions requiring management attention.

An assistant could assemble authorised information: the equipment manual, repair history, an approved procedure and parts availability. It could then draft a work request or a concise summary. Each claim should carry its source, currency and a clear status: confirmed, suspected or still being checked.

If a spare part is unavailable, the system may help prepare an enquiry. A qualified specialist must approve any substitute. Emergency actions follow approved procedures. A language model must not invent hazardous instructions or send control commands to equipment.

A useful outcome is a preserved history: who reported the event, who checked the cause, what decision was approved and how the work ended. This is a concept for possible use, not a claim of deployment at any factory.

## From an article to a responsible pilot

An organisation can begin with a limited scope: searching approved documents, drafting maintenance requests and summarising events. Its existing portal retains user management. The assistant receives only the information it needs through authorised interfaces. The public VMESTE website must not gain access to industrial data.

The first pilot can be read-only. Local hosting can support processing without sending documents to a public model, but location alone does not ensure security. Access controls, audit records, backups and checks of what the system reads and stores are still necessary.

Benefits should be assessed against questions chosen beforehand. How long does retrieval take? Is the correct source cited? Can a worker see someone else's restricted documents? Does the model invent the cause of a fault? Can service be restored after a failure? Hardware and user capacity must follow measured workloads rather than a compelling presentation.

After the pilot, specialists decide what to improve and whether to expand. Employees' experience becomes reviewed knowledge, while AI helps retrieve and organise it. Engineering decisions, work authorisations and responsibility remain with people.
