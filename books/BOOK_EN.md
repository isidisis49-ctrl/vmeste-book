# VMESTE — From One Website to a World of Technology

*An illustrated story of three months of building*

## It All Happened Unexpectedly

The original plan was simple: build a website where people could ask for help and offer services. But each solved problem raised another question. Soon there were ideas for AI assistants, engineering simulations and a library of practical tools. None of this was planned as a vast technology ecosystem.

*The first lesson: remarkable journeys can begin with one small decision.*

## VMESTE: Where It All Began

The platform was designed around requests, responses, journeys and everyday services. Gradually it gained a server application, databases, maps, user accounts and backups. A useful service needs more than attractive screens: it must protect information and behave predictably.

*Example: someone posts a request to move household items; another person may respond, discuss the details and offer help.*

## VESTA: AI That Helps People Explain What They Need

Not everyone wants to navigate a complicated form. VESTA aims to turn an ordinary description into an editable request and ask useful follow-up questions. Local tests use language models and a reference catalogue. A catalogue listing is not proof that a provider has joined the platform or is available.

*Example: “The kitchen tap is leaking.” VESTA can clarify the problem and draft a request without inventing an available plumber.*

## TASHI: An Assistant on Your Own Computer

Work on the website inspired another project: a Windows assistant designed to use local speech recognition, work with files and launch applications. Offline operation and straightforward installation are important goals. The software is still being developed and tested.

*Possible future scenario: ask the assistant to find a drawing, open related documents and prepare a task list.*

## From Software to Engineering

The work also branched into physical systems: a controller design, printed-circuit-board preparation in KiCad and a heating simulator. An authentic screenshot in this book shows a model of a building with five entrances, multiple floors and coloured temperature indicators. These are simulated values, not readings from installed equipment.

*Example: compare the temperatures of different zones and spot deviations before building a physical device.*

## A Library of Everyday Tools

A separate interface brought together practical categories: calculations, cars, motorcycles, home, garden and fishing. The book includes a genuine screenshot of the catalogue. The idea is deliberately universal rather than tied to particular towns or regions.

*Example: find a materials calculator or vehicle-maintenance planning tool through one consistent interface.*

## Local AI for Organisations

Another direction is a general concept for an on-premises AI assistant. It could organise event logs, retrieve verified knowledge and prepare draft reports. Human specialists must approve decisions and safety-critical actions. No organisation, internal documentation or infrastructure is identified here.

*Example: a specialist records a fault; the assistant assembles an initial report and relevant approved information for review.*

## Robots: A Look Ahead

Once voice AI, sensors and automation enter the picture, an exciting question follows: could they help physical robots? We imagine a safe workshop arm, a mobile inspection helper and systems for repetitive tasks. These are research ideas and artistic visions, not robots already built.

*Possible research example: a robot collects sensor readings while a qualified person remains responsible for decisions.*

## The Workshop Nobody Planned

One website led to a surprising thought: perhaps these projects could someday become a small technology workshop spanning web software, AI, electronics, calculators and engineering models. It is a vision for future development, not a claim that a company has already been formed.

*After three months, the greatest assets were not just code: they included experience, prototypes, test discipline and the determination to continue.*

## What Were Those Three Months Worth?

It is natural to want to understand the value of months of work. A preliminary figure of 3–10 million roubles was discussed as a rough reproduction-cost range for part of the website’s technical foundation. It is not an independent valuation or the market value of a business. The real achievement is that an idea acquired a working foundation.

*VMESTE is only the beginning. The next chapters must be written through real results, not promises.*
